Love-Driven Development is the name pjdhiro wants to give to what drives projects — business, life and family — before a vision is put into words: a driving source pjdhiro wants to call Love, and the structures and processes it gives rise to. It starts from two questions about the driving source, its what and its why: "What drives us to do projects? What drives us to live?" and "Why do we try to become something different from what we were?" The hypothesis is that the driving source is "what I want to call Love". The first half (Parts 1–5) has the driving source as its theme and answers the first question; the second half (Parts 6–7) has development as its theme and heads toward the second. Part 1 sorts the driving sources seen by management studies into seven groups and looks at what the word Love has pointed to. Part 2 likens the invisible driving source to the five stages of creation (field → wave → edge → vortex → bundle, with cases from physics, life science, chemistry, psychology, romantic love and management studies; words as names for vortices seen between the waves) and to gravity (the gravity well), and gathers how far it resembles physics in structure. Part 3 looks at how people cut out the world with signals of survival and trust, how signals well up from inside the body, how a lack (kesson) is felt when signals and thoughts part, and how value is born in the consciousness of the receiver. Part 4 asks what trust is and what reassurance is: it sets out five premises of the mind (the unconscious, the sources of trust and love, defense, holding, stages of development), then compares trust, through four viewpoints of psychology, with expectation and acceptance (self-actualization; self-acceptance as trust in oneself), anxiety, faith and idealization (defense mechanisms; anxiety as what wells up when protection peels away), reassurance, dependence and attachment (the mother–child relationship) and the classification of emotions, and lines up outlines of trust and reassurance. Part 5 looks at what pjdhiro wants to call Love and answers that the driving source of projects may be the lack felt as the gap between Love and oneself. Part 6 asks what development points to (5W1H), what the view is good for (arranging the input), where the words of business are misunderstood (vision, psychological safety, GRIT, resilience, intrinsic motivation, negative capability, conflict, entrepreneurship, value creation), how design comes in (types of thinking and Baldwin and Clark's design rules), and what is still not known. Part 7 looks at practice: the situations where the view is useful (those who commission projects; processing anxiety in a mature form rather than by defense; readers outside Japan interested in Zen, meditation or mindfulness), training as removing bugs from the system of the mind (knowing the system through shikan and psychology, and loosening defense to accept weakness), and Zen and the Heart Sutra. The back matter places existentialism (under construction), terms, background and history, sources and references, and links. What I Want to Call Love-Driven Development β ver. 1.037 (ja)

What drives us to do projects?
Why do we try to become something different from what we were?

This reader is pjdhiro's own answer to these two existential questions.

Overview

BackgroundOver this year, pjdhiro has explored the shapes things take as they come into being, and human consciousness. Along the way, a question remained: what moves them? pjdhiro has also seen people's minds acting on one another unconsciously, and movements of emotion that do not proceed by logic. (For more, see "How I came to this question" at the end.)

QuestionWhat drives us to do projects? Why do we try to become something different from what we were?On this page, whatever moves people and projects is called the driving source. The two questions ask the “what” and the “why” of the driving source. Projects are not limited to business; they include life and family. It can feel as though something had begun to move before the vision was put into words.

HypothesisThat driving source may be “what I want to call Love”.

GroundsLove-Driven Development is pjdhiro's theory from plain experience. The grounds behind its claims are one person's plain experience, not statistics from studying and counting many people. The research cited is lined up not to prove the experience but as material for conveying it. Where experience and research disagree, neither is made to fit the other; the disagreement is left as a question.

StructureThen what is it? This reader explores that. Because it is hard to put into words, the knowledge and views needed to convey it are laid out in order.
  • The views so far: checks the views management studies has held on the driving source of projects. Each is a right aspect, but they alone feel as if something is missing. It also looks at what the word Love has pointed to so far.
  • Seeing through similar shapes: what I want to call Love cannot be seen with the eyes. The invisible driving source is looked at through things of similar shape: the structure of how things come into being, and gravity. It also gathers the shapes borrowed from physics and looks at how far the structure resembles them.
  • How do people interpret the world?: looks at the inside of the person. People interpret the world while sensing with the body whether they can stay alive (survival) and whether they can stay connected to others (trust).
  • How does the mind feel trust and reassurance?: the feeling called Love resembles trust and reassurance, and they have been spoken of overlapping. So this part answers two questions, what is trust and what is reassurance. It sets down premises of the mind, looks at differences from similar things by viewpoint of psychological theory, and lines up their outlines. It also looks again through the lenses of stages and personality. 🚧
  • What is Love?: with all this as preparation, looks at what this reader wants to call Love, and answers the first question. This is the first half of the reader, whose theme is the driving source. 🚧
  • What is happening inside what is called development?: from here is the second half of the reader, whose theme is development. It heads toward the second question. It looks at what the word development points to, and at words used within it (vision, the words of the mind, value creation, design) with the views so far. An exploration from the viewpoint of computer science and AI is also planned here. 🚧
  • Practice: suppose the reasoning is understood; now, what to do? After looking at the situations in which to use this view, it goes on to how to train the skill and how to practice, and also looks through the viewpoint of Zen. 🚧
  • Back matter: where it stands from the viewpoint of existentialism (🚧), words on this page, background and history, sources and references, and links to pjdhiro's pages.

Answer for nowWe may be inside something like a field we want to call Love, and part of it. The body senses the reassurance of being connected, and gaps from it. Feeling those gaps as a lack, people start to move. The driving source of projects may be this lack, the gap between what I want to call Love and oneself. The use of this view is to check what one has received before applying theories or instructions.
