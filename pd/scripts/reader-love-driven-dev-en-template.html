This is the English version of "What I Want to Call Love-Driven Development β", translated by Claude from Japanese version 1.234 on 2026-11-18 (the Japanese version, love-driven-dev.html, may be newer). Love-Driven Development is the name pjdhiro wants to give to what drives projects (business, life and family) before a vision is put into words: a driving source pjdhiro wants to call Love, and the structures and processes it gives rise to. It starts from two plain questions about the driving source, its what and its why: "What drives us to do projects? What drives us to live?" and "Why do we try to become something different from what we were?" The hypothesis is that the driving source is "what I want to call Love"; pjdhiro does not mean only agape or creative love, but feels something like Love at the source of every sense of the word. The answer for now: we may be inside something like a field we want to call Love, and part of it; the driving source of projects may be the lack felt as the gap between it and oneself. The view is pursued in the belief that it contributes to creation (including design and growth), to asking questions, and to value judgment and decision-making. The first half (Parts 1–5) has the driving source as its theme and answers the first question; the second half (Parts 6–7) has development as its theme and heads toward the second. Part 1 (the question and the views so far) sorts the driving sources seen by management studies into seven groups, sets out what Love-Driven Development sees as the driving source, and looks at what the word Love has pointed to. Part 2 (seeing the invisible driving source through similar shapes) opens with a guide to the part, then looks at the five stages of creation (field → wave → edge → vortex → bundle, with cases from physics, life science, chemistry, psychology, romantic love and management studies; words as names for vortices seen between the waves), at gravity (the gravity well), and at how far it resembles physics in structure. Part 3 (how people interpret the world) opens with a guide, then looks at signals of survival and trust, signals welling up from inside the body, the lack (kesson) felt when signals and thoughts part, and value born in the consciousness of the receiver. Part 4 (how the mind feels trust and reassurance) opens with a guide (the feeling called Love resembles trust and reassurance), sets out premises of the mind (the unconscious, where feelings of trust and love come from, defense, when defense loosens, stages of development), asks why feelings similar to trust are hard to tell apart, compares trust by four viewpoints of psychology with expectation and acceptance, anxiety, faith and idealization, reassurance, dependence and attachment, and the classification of emotions, and lines up outlines of trust and reassurance, seen again through stages and personality. Part 5 (what is Love) looks at what pjdhiro wants to call Love, what it is not, its aspects, analogies, its likeness to Peirce's agapasm (under construction), and answers that the driving source of projects may be the lack felt as the gap between Love and oneself. Part 6 (what is happening inside what is called development) asks what development points to (5W1H, with an as-is/to-be figure), how the words of development can be checked with this view, where the words of business are misunderstood (vision, the words of the mind such as psychological safety, GRIT, resilience, intrinsic motivation, negative capability, conflict and entrepreneurship, and value creation), where design comes in, what changes when the input is arranged, the answer of the second half, and what is still not known. Part 7 (what I want to call Love-Driven Development) gives the definition (under construction), supplements it from the viewpoints of education (peer learning), computer science (under construction), philosophy (pragmatism, pure experience, existentialism, the Eastern classics) and Zen, and turns to practice: what the view is good for and in what situations it is useful (including the skills of 2030 and Teal organizations), and how to train it and practice (removing bugs from the system of the mind). The back matter has words on this page, where it looks different from other people's theories (a table, under construction), background and history, how the collaboration with AI proceeds, sources and references, and links. About this page

Overview

QuestionWhat drives us to do projects? Why do we try to become something different from what we were?On this page, whatever moves people and projects is called the driving source. Projects are not limited to business; they include life and family. These questions remained over a year in which pjdhiro explored how things come into being and human consciousness (“How I came to this question”).

HypothesisThat driving source may be “what I want to call Love”. Many definitions have been given to the word Love. As to which of them is meant, pjdhiro sees it as follows.

pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):It is not that I want to call only agape, or only creative love, Love. Even in the narrowest sense, I feel something like Love at its source. It is a great flow, like the sea or the wind, behind the vortices between the waves. Even a part of it happens self-similarly, and I think the part and the whole are something alike. The word Love may have been interpreted the way the blind men describe the elephant. Isn't one of the reasons we have gone without revisiting the definition of the word Love that every one of them is right?

Answer for nowWe may be inside something like a field we want to call Love, and part of it. The body senses the reassurance of being connected, and gaps from it. Feeling those gaps as a lack, people start to move. The driving source of projects may be this lack, the gap between what I want to call Love and oneself.

What it is good forThis view is pursued in the belief that it contributes to creation, including design and growth, to asking questions, and to value judgment and decision-making. All of these are seen as especially needed in the age of AI. Its uses and the situations where it helps are looked at in “What is this view good for, and in what situations is it useful?”, and how to train it in “How does one train it, how does one practice?”.

StructureThe first half (Parts 1 to 5) looks at the first question, and the second half (Parts 6 and 7) at the second. The parts are, in order, The question, and the views so far · Seeing the invisible driving source through similar shapes · How do people interpret the world? · How does the mind feel trust and reassurance? · What is Love? · What is happening inside what is called development? · What I want to call Love-Driven Development, followed by the back matter.

GroundsLove-Driven Development is pjdhiro's theory from plain experience. Its grounds are one person's plain experience, not statistics from studying and counting many people. Research is cited not to prove the experience but as material for conveying it, and where it disagrees with the experience, the disagreement is left as a question.
