# What I Want to Call Love-Driven Development β

<!--PART:Part 1|The question, and the views so far-->

## What drives a project?

<!--OPEN-->

After a year of exploring the shapes things take as they come into being, and human consciousness, one question remained.

<a id="ldd-question"></a><strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>What drives us to do projects? What drives us to live?

Businesses and projects are usually told starting from a vision. Yet looking back, it can feel as though something had begun to move before the vision was put into words. Even the people making something sometimes cannot tell whether they are making it or it is arising from what surrounds them. <a id="ldd-drive"></a>On this page, whatever moves people and projects is called the **driving source**, and the page looks for what it is.

This question asks what the driving source is. Rereading this reader, pjdhiro said that what belongs at the opening may be two questions about the driving source, its "what" and its "why", and added the question on the side of why.

<a id="ldd-question-why"></a><strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>Why do we try to become something different from what we were?

With this "try to become", pjdhiro wants to say, without separating them, both the passive sense of becoming without meaning to and the active sense of seeming to try to become. The first half of this reader (Parts 1 to 5) looks at the first question, and the second half (Parts 6 and 7) at the second.

<details><summary>More — what starts moving before a vision is put into words</summary>

The movement before a vision is put into words takes several forms. Within one person, dots may connect afterward, as if preparation had already begun. Often the field itself is fluctuating, as with social movements and trends, and people start to move riding that fluctuation, or sometimes betting against it. People being drawn together and something beginning is one case among these.

About the results of design and vision, pjdhiro feels the following.

<strong>pjdhiro's experience (translated by Claude):</strong>…It seems as if I am trying to make it, but it is born as if the field were swirling into a vortex. I feel so many driving sources that I cannot tell whether it is me or the environment. Is it causation? Is it correlation?

</details>

<details><summary>More — the path management studies have written, and what is not yet known</summary>

Management studies and project management have described in detail the path that follows once a vision has been put into words: set the purpose, make a plan, gather people and resources, check progress and correct course. Such descriptions work as they are in many situations. Which way of thinking has treated what as the driving source is listed in the next chapter, "[What has management studies seen as the driving source?](#ldd-management)".

About the vision itself, the 5W1H are still not well understood: why it arises, and where, in whom, when, from what and how. Whether business opportunities are discovered or created is still debated in entrepreneurship research (in 2000 Shane and Venkataraman framed the field around the discovery, evaluation and exploitation of opportunities, and in 2007 Alvarez and Barney set side by side two views, discovery and creation).

Looking back, many people have experienced dots connecting before a vision, as if preparation had already begun, or being moved by fluctuations in the field. What Love-Driven Development tries to see is something like a law that works on people, beyond their control, behind such things happening. For now it approaches the "why" of this from the angle of the driving source.

## What has management studies seen as the driving source?

<!--OPEN-->

When explaining Love-Driven Development, pjdhiro has put answering the following question at the core of the explanation.

<a id="ldd-management"></a><strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>What is the driving source of a project?

Management studies has seen the driving source in many places: markets and competition, a company's resources, goals and plans, learning from action, organizational culture, people's motivation. Each way of thinking works well in situations that suit it. Yet laid on top of one another, they feel like the parable of the blind men and the elephant: each surely touches part of the elephant, and still something seems missing. Going down from the outside inward, one finally arrives at the entrepreneur's mind. What Love-Driven Development follows is what lies further behind that mind and moves it.

<details><summary>More — seven groups by what is seen as the driving source</summary>

The table below sorts representative ways of thinking into seven groups by what they see as the driving source. They are ordered from the external environment toward the inside of the person, and the right-hand column gives the situations where each works well.

<div class="tablefit cols-3">

| Driving source | Representative ways of thinking | Situations where it works |
|---|---|---|
| External environment (markets, competition, customers, stakeholders, the wider environment) | Shareholder value maximization, competitive strategy, disruptive innovation, marketing (Kotler), stakeholder theory, the environmental school | Right as long as the forces coming from outside line up with the direction people are drawn in |
| Internal resources | Resource-based view (VRIO) | Right as long as the people who make use of the resources are drawn to them |
| An endpoint decided in advance (goals, plans, efficiency) | Management by objectives (MBO), OKR, business planning, scientific management, lean production | Right as long as one does not ask where the endpoint came from |
| Action and learning | Lean startup, real options, emergent strategy | Right as long as what to test and where to move keep arising |
| What a group shares (norms, culture, purpose) | Business ethics, compliance, CSR, organizational culture, knowledge creation theory (Nonaka), purpose-driven management | Right as long as the group is open and what is shared keeps arising |
| The inside of the person (empathy, motivation, judgment) | Design thinking, motivation theories, rational choice | Right as long as one does not ask where empathy and motivation come from |
| The entrepreneur's mind (vision, desire, instinct) | The entrepreneurial school, effectuation | Right as long as one does not ask what moves that mind |

</div>

When people look only at the part they touched and think the whole elephant is there, words turn into hollow buzzwords. What Love-Driven Development tries to do is touch the parts not yet touched.

In the entrepreneur's mind, vision, desire and instinct all surely work as driving sources. The careers of Kotler and Ikujiro Nonaka, who kept updating their theories for decades, also seem to show their center of gravity shifting toward people's feelings and values.

A table that looks at each way of thinking in the table, by the situations where it works well and the misreadings likely when it is spread as the right answer, is in [How far do the ideas of management studies work?](management-drivers.html) (in Japanese).

## What does Love-Driven Development see as the driving source?

<!--OPEN-->

The ways of thinking in management studies have each seen something as the driving source. Then how does Love-Driven Development differ from them?

<a id="ldd-what"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Until now, the concepts of a project have been grouped in ways like purpose-driven development, motivation-driven development, vision-driven development. I think I went one step further and looked for why those arise.

<a id="ldd-answer"></a>The driving source of Love-Driven Development is what I want to call Love. On this page, Love is not limited to romantic love, and for now it is placed in two aspects: the aspect of a force by which people and things feel drawn, and the aspect of a property of the field that people are in. pjdhiro sees both as right, and each as one aspect, like what each of the men who touched the elephant in the poem of the previous chapter said. The analogy is the relation between gravity and spacetime. Gravity is felt as a force that draws things, and in general relativity it is also written as a property of the field, the curvature of spacetime (the chapter "[Isn't Love like gravity?](#ldd-well)"). The driving sources in [the management studies table](#ldd-management) were things of a size people can see with their eyes and talk about in words, like markets, goals, motivation and the entrepreneur's mind. What I want to call Love is what moves those from behind them. Love-Driven Development is the name pjdhiro wants to give, all together, to what is overlooked on the back side of the phenomenon called a project: this driving source, and the structures and processes it gives rise to.

<details><summary>More — what moves behind, before a vision is put into words</summary>

Something begins to be born while there is not yet a vision put into words or a shared intention. Within one person, in a group of people, and in the flow of society, such movements happen before a vision is put into words.

In Part 5, the chapter "[What aspects does what I want to call Love have?](#ldd-love-faces)" looks at these two aspects alongside other aspects.

</details>

<details><summary>More — how to point to something that cannot be seen</summary>

Answering "It is Love" conveys almost nothing. What lies before a vision cannot be observed directly. The word love, too, is easily read through its easily recognized aspects, such as romance and liking.

<a id="ldd-overlap"></a>So this page writes the way physics has written about gravity. It piles up phenomena that are moved by what I want to call Love, and the words that have described them. Each one, like a circle, encloses a certain range. When many circles are laid over one another, the overlap common to all of them remains: in a Venn diagram, the AND of all the circles. That overlap shows where what I want to call Love is.

<a id="ldd-four"></a>The circles this page lays over one another are mainly four explorations. Over this year, pjdhiro has continued "[What Is Creation](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/cs/)", "[What Is Awareness](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/as/)" and "[Kesson-Driven Thinking](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/ks/)" (in Japanese), and the reader "[Isn't Love like gravity?](gravity-love.html)" (in Japanese). Like gravity, each is an exploration that has described, from a different side, how the things being moved move. This page calls them the **four explorations**, and calls on them one at a time where needed in the flow of the story.

</details>

<details class="pd-column"><summary>The finger pointing at the moon</summary>

<a id="ldd-finger"></a>pjdhiro thinks that the more clearly something is expressed, the more it is misunderstood one-sidedly. As in the Buddhist parable of the finger pointing at the moon (the finger pointing at the moon is not the moon itself; *Śūraṅgama Sūtra* fascicle 2, *Dà zhìdù lùn* fascicle 9), the words and hypotheses on this page are not what I want to call Love itself, but fingers pointing at it.

</details>

<details><summary>More — gravity has been described by the things it moves</summary>

There is a precedent for describing what cannot be seen: physics. No one has seen gravity itself. Even so, it has been inferred that a falling apple and the orbiting moon follow the same law, and, from how gas and stars on the outskirts of galaxies rotate, that there is mass not visible in light. Gravity has been described by how the things it moves move. In the image of a black hole (published in 2019), the center itself does not appear either. What appears is a dark shadow and the ring of light that rims it. Where the center is can be told from the surrounding light.

<strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>Am I talking about the structure of a project, or a viewpoint for looking at projects, or the principle of why projects are born and move, or a design process?

Placed side by side, the four explorations do not fit into any single one of these. The driving source is close to the principle of why things are born and move. <details class="pd-column"><summary>What this page writes, and how</summary>

<a id="ldd-method"></a>pjdhiro has experienced many times the phenomenon pjdhiro wants to call Love-Driven Development. From that experience, this page puts into words a viewpoint, a stance and a way of working toward projects. What it deals with is the driving source of projects. Projects are not limited to business; they include life and family. Such human activities are a domain sociology has broadly taken as its subject, and management studies and economics have dealt with part of it. This page reads that domain from the angle of the driving source, from the viewpoint of psychology. In doing so, it draws on structural similarities with physics (gravity and quantum theory) and on findings from neurophenomenology (a way of looking that sets descriptions of experience against findings on how nerves work).

Put another way: when a project moves, people are thinking and feeling something. This page looks at that in light of how nerves work. Nerves, too, are made of the same matter as everything else in the universe and follow the same laws of physics. So when the way thinking and feeling move is set beside the behavior of gravity and quanta, there are places where the shapes look similar. This page uses those similar shapes as clues to infer the invisible driving source.

## What has the word Love pointed to?

<!--OPEN-->

Even saying "what I want to call Love", what the word Love points to differs by person and era. When looking at what Love is, pjdhiro first asked Claude this.

<a id="ldd-love-history"></a><strong>pjdhiro's request (translated by Claude):</strong>So that it's clear I'm dealing with LOVE in a broad range, first sort out the existing kinds of Love.

The words that have been translated as Japanese *ai* or English love were different words in the sources. What they are written as is not one thing either: a movement of the mind, a mind or practice to be cultivated, a desire seeking what is lacking, a relationship, a principle or force that moves the world, God itself. What they are directed at also differs from source to source: from one particular person to all living beings, God, the makeup of the world. Nor have they been written only as something good; they have also been written as the root of suffering and the seed of deeds deserving punishment. Modern dictionaries line these up under one word, which shows that the single word Love has been applied to a very broad range. Who called what Love, and when, is gathered in detail, from the full text of the sources, on the separate page "[How has Love been spoken of?](love-history.html)" (in Japanese; under construction), in the order of the Buddhist stream, the stream of Western philosophy, and modern dictionaries, psychology and neuroscience. For now, in pjdhiro's view, what this page wants to call Love is closest to what Peirce described as "[evolution by creative love](peirce-love-abduction.html)" (in Japanese; under construction).

<details><summary>More — words, ways of writing, objects, good and bad, dictionaries</summary>

What is lined up here is Love as defined by people other than pjdhiro. What pjdhiro wants to call Love is seen from the Part 5 chapter "[What I want to call Love](#ldd-love)" on.

**It was not one word.** The words that have been translated as Japanese *ai* or English love are different words in the sources. In early Buddhism, craving (taṇhā), held to be the cause of suffering, and loving-kindness (mettā), held to be a mind to be cultivated, are different words. In Greek, eros is used by Plato, friendship (philia) by Aristotle and agape by the New Testament: different sources. The *Mencius* distinguishes three words, *ai*, *ren* and *qin*, by whom they are directed at.

**What it is written as is not one thing either.** It is not limited to what is written as an emotion.

<div class="tablefit cols-2">

| Written as | Examples |
|---|---|
| A movement or state of the mind | Craving that becomes the root of suffering (early Buddhism). Joy accompanied by the idea of an external cause (Spinoza). A state of intense longing for union with another (Hatfield and Sprecher) |
| A mind or practice to be cultivated | A mind extended without limit to all living beings (the *Metta Sutta*). A mind that gives happiness and removes suffering (*Dà zhìdù lùn*). A practice that comes out as words (Dōgen's loving speech). A commandment (the New Testament) |
| A desire seeking what is lacking | Socrates' dialogue in Plato's *Symposium* |
| A relationship | Friendship wishing each other good (Aristotle). The child's tie to the mother (Bowlby) |
| A principle or force that moves the world | The working that gathers the four roots into one (Empedocles). The love that moves the sun and the other stars (Dante). A mode of evolution (Peirce) |
| A weight | The weight that carries oneself (Augustine) |
| God itself | "God is love" (the First Epistle of John) |
| A combination of components | The three of intimacy, passion and decision/commitment (Sternberg) |

</div>

**What it is directed at also differs from source to source.** One particular person, someone with whom one wishes each other good, those extended in order from the near, all living beings without boundaries, God, things that are not human, oneself, the makeup of the world.

**Nor has it been written only as something good.** The craving of early Buddhism is the root from which suffering arises. The *Dhammapada* writes that from what is dear arise sorrow and fear. Dante writes that love is the seed both of every virtue and of every deed deserving punishment. Augustine writes that two loves made two cities.

**Modern dictionaries line these up under one word.** The *Daijisen* divides *ai* into six: the affection of parents and children or siblings, the heart that holds a particular person dear, the feeling of liking things, a heart wishing for happiness beyond personal feeling, the loving-kindness of God in Christianity, and the craving of Buddhism. *Ren'ai* (romantic love) is a word used as the translation of love from the beginning of the Meiji era. Psychology has divided love into measurable forms such as behavior, states and components. Neuroscience research measures the brain activity of people in love, but does not write that some part of the brain is the seat of love.

</details>

## What shape does creation take?

<!--OPEN-->

Part 2 first looks at the structure of how things come into being. One of the explorations, "What Is Creation", writes about that structure. The shape of creation continues as a basic viewpoint into the second half of this reader, so an overview is placed first.

"What Is Creation" holds that creation proceeds field → wave → edge → vortex → bundle. Its top page sets out the five stages like this: "The field is the environment and conditions in which creation arises; the wave is the process of exploration and fluctuation; the edge is the boundary where heterogeneous elements meet; the vortex is the core stage in which elements combine and transform; the bundle is the completion in which they are integrated as a new form" (translated by Claude).

pjdhiro also rephrases the five stages with an image of the sea. In the sequence sea → wave → trough between waves → vortex → ocean current, the field is the sea, the wave is the wave, the edge is the trough between waves, the vortex is the vortex, and the bundle is the ocean current (the "sea analogy" column of the [table of the five stages](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/cs/reader/five-stages.html) in "What Is Creation", in Japanese). The trough of the edge corresponds to the border where heterogeneous things meet, and the ocean current of the bundle to a gathering that remains as a new form and becomes the next field.

A field appears, waves rise, different things meet at an edge, bind as a vortex, and become a new form as a bundle. Shapes like this have been reported in [30 academic domains](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/cs/?modal=domains) (in Japanese).

<a id="ldd-field"></a>From here on, the word "field" is used for both the field of the five stages of creation and the field of physics: what spreads beneath, where a vortex swirls.

pjdhiro sees this shape of creation as closest to the image of a great ocean and its waves. pjdhiro also sees the vortex as the fluctuation of the field itself taking shape, something that cannot be cut out of the field and taken out.

Here are examples from five domains where similar shapes have been reported. The sources do not write in five stages; arranging the descriptions of the theories in the order of the five stages is Claude's reading.

- Physics (dissipative structures, with Bénard convection as the example): The field is a layer of liquid heated from below. Small fluctuations away from the average state keep arising and dying out. The wave is some fluctuations growing once the temperature gradient exceeds a certain value. The edge is the contest between buoyancy and the viscosity and heat conduction that hold it back; once the value is exceeded, of the many patterns of disturbance, the one that grows fastest wins out. The vortex is the grown fluctuation becoming an order sustained by exchange of energy with the outside, the layer dividing into cells. The bundle is scattered polygons slowly settling into a network of regular hexagons.
- Biochemistry (two, from life science and chemistry):
  - Fertilization (life science; from research on mouse zygotes): The field is the inside of the egg where fertilization happens. The wave is the half genomes (maternal and paternal) that the two gametes, split into female and male, the egg and the sperm, each bring. The edge is the chromosomes of the two genomes meeting for the first time when the nuclear membrane breaks down and the first division begins. The vortex is a spindle forming around each of the two genomes, the two lining up and carrying out one division together while the two genomes remain distinguishable without mixing. The bundle is becoming a two-cell embryo, and the two genomes mixing in the following divisions to become the genome of a new individual.
  - Chemistry (the BZ reaction): The field is the solution in one vessel (bromate ions, an organic acid and a catalyst dissolved in it). The wave is two substances competing for an intermediate. The edge is the switch to another process when one falls below a critical concentration, a process that indirectly brings it back. The vortex is neither process winning outright, and the back-and-forth remaining as a stable form. The bundle is arriving at the same single periodic orbit regardless of the initial state.
- Psychology (moving between good and bad aspects; Klein): The field is the infant's mind. The wave is love and hate splitting between times of being satisfied and not, and protecting oneself from the terrifying aspect by enlarging the good aspect (idealization). The edge is the two aspects held apart colliding, and fear of loss, a sense of guilt and feelings close to mourning arising. The vortex is the loved aspect and the hated aspect being felt as belonging to one person who is not split apart. The urge to repair the one one feels one has harmed (reparation) comes to the fore, trust is regained little by little, and while bearing the knowledge that the other was not perfect, one can keep trust and love. The bundle is the splitting movement remaining in changed form, and anxious situations being experienced again and again.
- A plain example (romantic love): The field is two people who do not know each other, each in their own life. The wave is the two meeting and being attracted to each other. The edge is, the closer they get, differences in ways of thinking and living, and the difference between the partner one pictured and the partner in front of one, colliding. The vortex is the two becoming as if one while each remains a different person. The bundle is the life and promises the two built up together remaining and becoming the field of the days to come. This flow was not built from research; Claude wrote it as a plain form (research on couples at the start of marriage is in the fold below).
- Management studies (innovation, and the evolution of organizations):
  - Innovation (Schumpeter's new combinations): The field is the circular flow of an economy repeating familiar routes. The wave is the person who tries to leave the familiar route and do something new, the resistance of surrounding society, and resistance within oneself. The edge is pulling means of production away from old uses and fighting competitors head on. The vortex is newly combining and using what was pulled away (new combinations). The bundle is new combinations occurring in swarms, disrupting the existing system and forcing adaptation, with revolution and the absorption of its results alternating.
  - Innovation (organizational knowledge creation, Nonaka et al.): The field is the organization and the project (months to years). The wave is pairs that look contradictory, such as tacit and explicit knowledge, or order and chaos. The edge is routines, habits and cognitive frameworks breaking down through change from outside or difficult challenges from above (fluctuation), and a sense of crisis arising. The vortex is a concept being born in dialogue and, after justification, becoming an archetype. The bundle is the archetype moving to another department or level and starting the next cycle.
  - The evolution of organizations (March's exploration and exploitation): The field is the organization's knowledge (the code) and the individuals belonging to it. The wave is the exploration of new possibilities and the exploitation of known certainties. The edge is the clash of gains between the short and long term, and between individual and organizational knowledge; the code can learn only from individuals who deviate from it. The vortex is slow-learning individuals and turnover keeping diversity, and the knowledge of the code rising. The bundle is the balance between exploration and exploitation being kept, and the organization surviving and prospering.

Cases that departed from each shape are also on record. In Bénard convection, if the temperature gradient is smaller than the dividing value, fluctuations die out and things return to how they were. In a zygote, if the two spindles fail to line up, the result is an embryo of cells with two nuclei. In the BZ reaction, when conditions are off, no oscillation happens and it settles into a steady state. In the wave of new combinations, businesses that cannot adapt may perish. An organization leaning only toward exploration scatters immature ideas; one leaning only toward exploitation is trapped in a suboptimal stability.

<details class="pd-column"><summary>From classical physics to quantum theory</summary>

Physics, too, had a similar change of grip. At the end of the nineteenth century, the theories of mechanics, heat, light and electromagnetism had achieved great success, and some thought few problems remained. Following the small discrepancies that remained and peering inside the atom, everyday words such as position and speed did not work as they were. Even so, understanding that minute world became the explanation of the large world: why things are hard, why the sun keeps shining.

In 1900, Lord Kelvin, in a lecture, named "two clouds" hanging over the theories of heat and light. One was that no evidence could be found of the earth moving through the ether that was thought to carry light. The other was that the theory of equipartition of energy did not agree with the specific heats of gases and solids. In the same year, to explain the distribution of colors of light given off by hot bodies, Planck assumed that energy is exchanged in discrete lumps. The first cloud later became one of the problems that led to the theory of relativity, and the second, alongside Planck's work on thermal radiation, became one of the starting points of quantum theory.

In the world of quantum theory, the position and momentum of a particle cannot both be fixed as precisely as one likes at the same time (the uncertainty principle). Electrons behave both like grains and like waves. Intuitions built from everyday words are betrayed here again and again.

Even so, understanding this minute world explained the large world: atoms keeping their shape without collapsing (the uncertainty principle); things having volume and being hard even when many atoms gather (the Pauli exclusion principle); atoms binding into molecules (the chemical bond); hydrogen turning into helium at the center of the sun so that it keeps shining (the tunnel effect makes that reaction easier to occur). Semiconductors, and the computers we use now, are built on this understanding too.

<details class="pd-column"><summary>The feeling of heat was the motion of molecules</summary>

There is a more everyday example too. The feeling of hot is a word of the large world, received with the body. <details class="pd-column"><summary>Particles were ways the field vibrates</summary>

In the minute world of physics, too, the way names are given has something in common with vortices between the waves. <details><summary>More — the four explorations and the shapes of physics</summary>

**The driving source and gravity.** In the figure of the gravity well, the things around revolve along the hollow made by the mass at the center.

**Kesson and the phase of waves.** In quantum mechanics, the state of a particle is written as a complex-valued wave (the wave function). The probability of finding the particle at a place is set by the square of the absolute value of that wave, and the direction of the wave (its phase) does not enter. Shifting the phase of the whole wave uniformly changes nothing in the observed results. But when two waves overlap, the difference in phase shows up clearly as interference fringes. The phase itself does not show up in numbers on its own, yet where waves overlap it produces a definite difference.

This is where it overlaps with the shape of kesson-driven thinking: a value that is 0 in cognition and yet works. What overlaps is the behavior. A value on the imaginary axis is a magnitude whose projection onto the axis of action is 0. Phase is a direction that disappears when the absolute value is taken. The reasons they do not show up in numbers differ, but they share not showing up in numbers on their own and yet working.

**Awareness and observation.** In the quantum world, measuring can change the state of what is measured. For example, measuring position disturbs momentum (the error in measuring position and the disturbance given to momentum cannot both be made zero, but it is known that their product can be made smaller than the limit once thought). In models of measurement, during measurement the object being measured and a small part of the apparatus (the probe) are taken together as one system, and its change over time is written. After the measurement, only the probe side is measured and read off on the scale. The model writes only this far; it does not write that the observing side and the observed side become inseparable. This page sees this form, writing the two together during measurement, as an analogy overlapping with where metacognition, dividing oneself, ends up.

**Creation and the fluctuations of the field.** In quantum field theory, particles appear as one of the ways a field vibrates (an excitation). Separately, as a matter of fluids, in superfluid helium the flow around a vortex (circulation) appears only in fixed units. In fluids, there are examples where the vortex itself appears as a form that cannot be cut off from the fluid.

<details><summary>Taking the estimate literally — do quanta work in the nerves?</summary>

The human body, too, is made of matter, and it is hard to think of it as outside the laws of physics. However, how far quantum effects work in the processing of neural signals has not yet been answered even among researchers.

Quantum effects working inside living things are studied as quantum biology. For example, it is known that the tunnel effect, in which particles slip through barriers, is involved in enzyme reactions.

For the brain, there are several proposals. Penrose and Hameroff proposed that quantum processes proceed in the microtubules inside neurons, and that each time they spontaneously collapse under certain conditions, a moment of consciousness arises. Fisher proposed that the nuclear spins of phosphorus atoms may be able to hold quantum information for a long time in the brain. There are also proposals and calculations that quantum coherence inside ion channels may be involved in how ions pass through.

There are also calculations against. Tegmark estimated that in the brain at body temperature, quantum superpositions break down in an extremely short time, falling far short of the speed of neural signals. Current neuroscience writes the release and reception of neurotransmitters, and the electrical signals that follow, almost entirely in classical physics. Where things stand now is that whether quantum effects work in the processing of neural signals has been neither confirmed nor fully ruled out.

## Where do the feelings of trust and love come from?

<!--OPEN-->

People come to trust someone, or to like someone. Is that feeling born each time from looking at the person in front of them? pjdhiro's words in the previous chapter named, as sources bearing on the definitions of trust and love, Freud's transference, Klein's object relations and Jung's archetypes. Of the psychology that influenced pjdhiro, pjdhiro names Freud's transference first.

<a id="ldd-source"></a><strong>pjdhiro's impression (translated by Claude):</strong>The phenomenon of transference became a starting point for me to think, from experience, about emotional contagion in general.

Psychoanalysis has written that some of the feelings of trust and love come not only from the person in front of one, but also from patterns already within oneself. In 1912 Freud wrote that, from what they are born with and the influences of childhood, people acquire a pattern of under what conditions and how they love, and repeat it throughout life. Part of the pattern remains unknown to the person. When the need for love is not fully satisfied in reality, that pattern is turned toward new people one meets. Freud called its being turned toward the doctor in the setting of treatment transference. In the same paper Freud writes that emotional ties such as sympathy, friendship and trust, however pure they feel to the person, lead back, when traced, to sexual drives.

In 1946 Klein wrote that the infant receives its first object split into a good one that satisfies and a bad one that does not, and that this becomes the dividing line between love and hate. In 1917 Jung wrote that within transference, primordial images common to people, which cannot be explained by the person's memories, are also projected (the chapter "[What is the unconscious?](#ldd-unconscious)"). In the same paper Jung writes that when such images are projected onto people around one, they become excessive exaltation or contempt, producing infatuation and conflict. In a 1919 paper Jung calls inherited patterns of the mind by the word archetypes.

What the three have in common is the view that some of the feelings of trust and love come not only from the person in front of one, but from patterns one is not conscious of. The patterns the three describe differ: a version of childhood relationships (Freud), images split into good and bad (Klein), images common to people (Jung). Rather than deciding which is right, one can also see them, like the blind men and the elephant, as touching different aspects of the same thing.

## What looks threatening changes with the stage of development

<!--OPEN-->

When considering whether Love-Driven Development could be drawn as a diagram of a mechanism with inputs and outputs, pjdhiro also touched on how that mechanism differs from person to person.

<a id="ldd-stages"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>This system is partly hardware-dependent — what are called stages of development. The details and complexity it can handle differ by them. But the position of zero and the rough movement are the same. It looks like it will turn out to be something like that. An information-processing system dependent on human ability.

Research on adult development writes that the mechanism by which people make meaning of themselves and the world is reorganized several times over a lifetime, and has called each of these a stage (see also: [What are stages of mental development?](developmental-stages.html), in Japanese). Cook-Greuter writes that the main anxieties and the defenses often used change from stage to stage.

As an overview, Cook-Greuter writes that defenses include those that come from the reflexes of flight, fight and freeze against bodily threats, and mental ones such as denial, projection and rationalization, and that both are indispensable for survival and for coping with stress. When the meaning-making mechanism is disturbed, people deny or do not pay attention in order to stay safely within what they know, and the greater the threat, the more they harden their current view. It is written that the later the stage, the less need there is for defensive moves, but also that maladaptation and pathology can occur at any period.

## Why are feelings similar to trust hard to tell apart?

<!--OPEN-->

The chapters so far saw that the mind has sources the person cannot see, and that what looks threatening changes with the stage of development. From here, expectation, acceptance, anxiety, faith, idealization, reassurance and dependence, and attachment are grouped by viewpoint of theory and compared with trust. Why compare them? Can people, within themselves, tell trust apart from other feelings such as faith and expectation? pjdhiro sees the reason the word trust gets confused this way.

<a id="ldd-discern"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>The word trust is confusing because, depending on the subject's state and environment, it occurs in every kind of case. Even something others could not call trust, even something irrational, the subject ends up feeling as trust. Like faith.

Research has written that how far similar feelings can be told apart differs from person to person, and changes with growth even in the same person. Cook-Greuter writes that the fineness of the words for expressing feelings changes with each stage of ego development. The table below sums up that description stage by stage. The names of the stages are the same as in the table of the previous chapter.

<div class="tablefit cols-2">

| Stage | How feelings are expressed |
|---|---|
| Self-protective | Distinguishes only very simple feelings, such as feeling bad, upset, excited, angry. May express non-bodily things in bodily words |
| Conformist | Names "acceptable" feelings such as sad, happy, nervous, upset, without fine distinctions. References to inner feelings are stereotyped and follow cultural expectations. Negative feelings and disappointment are often countered by showing overly positive feelings and enthusiasm, and feelings such as anger are suppressed and rarely reach awareness |
| Expert | Words for states of mind begin to become a little finer. Matters of the mind are grasped in words of unchanging moods and traits. Shame and guilt are expressed in simple words or explained away |
| Achiever | Comes to distinguish the many aspects of being human much more finely. Separates appearance from feeling, what was said from what was not. Tries to explore feelings that do not fit one's current understanding rather than denying them or throwing them onto others. Keeps using those defenses, but the range of experience one can look at widens. Can express the feelings one distinguishes and own contradictory qualities ("I am an optimistic pessimist") |
| Pluralist | Notices that feelings spread throughout the body, and begins to notice that feelings, thoughts and bodily sensations affect one another |
| Strategist | Understands that ambivalent feelings are natural. Discerns fine differences between similar feelings, distinguishing sadness, grief, regret, disappointment and so on |
| Construct-aware | Begins to pay attention to the patterns of processing of one's own feelings and reasoning. Can feel any feeling, but questions the certainty and appropriateness of a feeling even as one feels or expresses it |

</div>

Reading the table from top to bottom, the way feelings are distinguished changes from very coarse to discerning fine differences between similar feelings. However, research measuring whether trust itself can be told apart from similar feelings has not yet been found.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>The problem is a matter of the degree of emotional metacognition, like: can one tell trust, faith and expectation apart?

Emotional metacognition is watching one's own emotions as objects. Even if they are hard to separate inside the person, what can be told apart from outside can be lined up. The chapters that follow take up the similar feelings grouped by viewpoint of psychological theory, and line up where they can be told apart from trust from outside. Each theory, like one of [the blind men feeling the elephant](#ldd-blind-elephant), touches one aspect of the elephant. Each time the viewpoint changes, another aspect of trust and reassurance comes into view.

## How do expectation and trust differ?

<!--OPEN-->

A word often mixed up with trust in everyday life is expectation. Is expectation a good thing, as something that moves projects?

<a id="ldd-expect"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Shinya Nitta poses the question "Is expectation bad?" and answers it. I agree from experience, and I think the kickoff comes after expectation is betrayed. After that, people despair, blame others, or accept.

In Shinya Nitta's theory, expectation is also called *ate* (counting on someone). It is the movement of the mind in which a self trying to be perfect counts on the other as omnipotent; Nitta counts it among the mind's moves to protect itself from discomfort, and also places it as the source of compulsion. About trust, Nitta writes of a different movement. Rather than hiding one's fragility and trying to be perfect, when the other supports that fragility, trust in the other wells up. Trust, Nitta writes, is born from forming relationships of mutual help.

pjdhiro gives this example of blaming others when expectations are not met in practice.

<a id="ldd-uncertain-practice"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>I think this is the cause of being told that the word business conception, or the concept of project design, is not as useful as one expects, or being sneered at with "that's not a vision, it's a delusion". I think it is an example of blaming others when "expectation" is not met in practice.

Seeing the other or a plan only in its good aspects is one of the places where vision is easily misunderstood, and we look at it in the section on vision in the chapter "[Where are the words of business misunderstood?](#ldd-vision)".

The working notes of "What Is Awareness" make a provisional arrangement that separates expectation and trust on two axes: the time they face and the content they contain. Expectation is a feeling facing ahead, picturing what will happen in the direction one hopes. In everyday language the two are mixed. pjdhiro sees expectation not as something that sharpens past experience or estimates of probability, but as energy for taking action. Where the image of expectation is betrayed looks as if it overlaps with the lack noticed through error against prediction, seen in the chapter "[When signals and thoughts part, one feels a lack](#ldd-kesson)".

pjdhiro says this about what the word trust is applied to.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>In what I call trust, I feel something like a vector heading toward stability and integration, and I apply the word to that.

That some things feel like trust to the person but cannot be called trust by others was seen in the chapter "[Why are feelings similar to trust hard to tell apart?](#ldd-discern)". Since what trust is is still being searched for, this page does not settle on one, and lines up several aspects.

## How do acceptance and trust differ?

<!--OPEN-->

The previous chapter saw that after an expectation is betrayed, people despair, blame others, or accept. How is that acceptance connected with trust? In a dialogue about acceptance, pjdhiro lines up two flows.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Kübler-Ross's "acceptance of death" sets the psychological changes of the dying as five stages: "denial", "anger", "bargaining", "depression", "acceptance". I think this fits quite well for "acceptance" other than of death too. And a person named Shinya Nitta holds that recovery from social withdrawal proceeds "expectation", "conceit", "meddling", "being betrayed", "resignation". Anger produces withdrawal. In bargaining, predictions of the future such as anticipation and wariness come in. When betrayed, one becomes depressed. Acceptance is achieved by giving up, by letting go. I think the two are similar.

The move from "being betrayed" to "resignation" in Nitta's sequence that pjdhiro cites corresponds to acceptance, among the three that follow a betrayed expectation (despair, blaming others, acceptance). pjdhiro later restated what these two sequences point to from the side of expectation and defense (on levels of defense, see the chapter "[The mind has mechanisms to protect itself](#ldd-defense)").

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Expectation is useful when starting to move, but it often ends in disappointment, and one is let down. But from there one sets out toward acceptance. I think this means psychological defense loosening and moving toward mature defense.

pjdhiro sees that attitude of acceptance as a healthy way of holding expectations, like this.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>An attitude that can accept even ending in disappointment (accepting reality) is probably the healthy way to hold expectations. That attitude gives others reassurance, and I think it is close to the word self-confidence. It does not become something that worries others and drags them in. However, using it cleverly can lead to attracting people, drawing them in, and using it in marketing to make money, so judging good and bad is difficult.

Acceptance is not only something that turns toward the other person or reality after an expectation fails. While rereading this reader, pjdhiro placed acceptance turned toward oneself at the start of self-actualization.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>I think self-acceptance is at the first position of self-actualization. You could also call it self-approval. You could also call it trust in oneself. Being convinced of oneself including what one cannot do and one's bad parts. That attitude has confidence. There is no pretense. A state that has accepted weakness. Because there is no defense of weakness, there is also little anxiety. To be precise, anxiety does arise, but one can be with the anxiety. I think this is one definition of courage. I think it is one definition of reassurance.

Maslow wrote that the need for self-actualization clearly emerges after the needs for physiology, safety, love and esteem are first satisfied, and also wrote that, about self-actualization, little is yet known either experimentally or clinically (1943). pjdhiro's view places at its start accepting oneself including what one cannot do and one's bad parts, and sees it both as trust in oneself and as one definition of reassurance. The healthy way of holding expectations in the previous chapter and this self-acceptance are both spoken of in words of confidence and reassurance.

pjdhiro also sees as acceptance the place in Klein's case seen in Part 2 where the loving side and the hating side come to be felt as belonging to one person who is not split apart (the chapter "[What shape does creation take?](#what-shape-does-creation-take)").

## What is anxiety?

<!--OPEN-->

<a id="ldd-uncertain"></a>The words on self-acceptance in the previous chapter included "to be precise, anxiety does arise, but one can be with the anxiety". Is anxiety something that should simply be erased? While thinking about where in this reader to treat the anxiety of not being able to see ahead, pjdhiro said this.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>The feeling of anxiety is very important. It is not the opposite of reassurance either.

On what anxiety is, pjdhiro cites one definition from the theory of the psychiatrist Shinya Nitta.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>I like Shinya Nitta's definition: something like the dissociation that happens when returning from a state of psychological defense to facing reality.

Nitta writes that "anxiety is precisely dissociation". Dissociation in Nitta's sense is a movement exactly opposite to the work that protects the mind (defense): the protection peels away, and the unpleasant self that had been protected comes out. Nitta writes that the two go back and forth, as "defense ⇔ dissociation", and writes of the movement of the protection peeling away as something everyone has. Read with this definition, anxiety wells up where the protection comes undone and one begins to face what one had not been looking at. The faith and idealization seen in this group are moves on the side of that protection.

🚧 How other theories besides Nitta's have defined anxiety is under construction (exploration: [#143](https://github.com/uminomae/project-design-theory/issues/143)).

A page that starts from the definition of anxiety and lines up its difference from fear, whether anxiety or protection comes first, whether one returns to protection or keeps facing, and how what anxiety faces differs by stage, is placed as the separate page "[What is anxiety?](anxiety.html)" (in Japanese).

<details><summary>More — how does anxiety well up when one cannot see ahead? (research on preparation and the feeling of safety)</summary>

When one cannot see ahead, the body first prepares, research has written. Some research grasps anxiety from the side of the uncertainty of future threat and of the workings that prepare for it (Grupe and Nitschke 2013). Some research also holds that under uncertainty the stress response is not born after something happens, but is working from the start, and is held down only while one feels safe (Brosschot, Verkuil and Thayer 2018). In this reading, the signal of survival is not peculiar to some person or some stage of growth, but comes back to anyone once safety can no longer be felt.

Where does the feeling of safety come from? The same research counts, as one source of the feeling of safety, a strong network of connections for people and for animals that live in groups (Brosschot, Verkuil and Thayer 2018). From here, the relation of the two signals can be re-read in one form: writing the signal of survival as what comes back when safety cannot be felt, and the signal of trust as the feeling of safety that holds it down. ["What Is Awareness"](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/as/) writes the two signals as two directions. Whether they are two separate signals, or one preparedness and what holds it down, touches the root of how "What Is Awareness" writes, so it has not been decided yet. Either way, the two are in the body at the same time and are not cut off from each other.

Grupe and Nitschke (2013) hold that uncertainty makes it difficult to prepare ahead and that this leads to anxiety, and describe moves to prepare for the uncertainty of future threat as adaptive while they are proportionate to the likelihood and severity of the threat.

Brosschot, Verkuil and Thayer (2018) placed the stress response as a default that is always on and is held down by the prefrontal cortex only when safety is felt. When safety can no longer be felt, that holding down comes off at once. They also write that the holding down coming off does not cause the stress response but allows it. As things that damage the feeling of safety, they name loneliness, low social status, urban environments and adversity in early childhood, and count a strong network of connections as one source of safety.

There is also a foothold for the reading that if not seeing ahead is held as a state known to be unseen, alarms are less likely to sound. Yu and Dayan (2005) separate the uncertainty of knowing that cues are unreliable within a familiar situation (expected uncertainty) from the uncertainty caused by an unannounced switch of situation (unexpected uncertainty), and propose assigning them to separate systems of neural modulation. In people with more than ten thousand hours of meditation, activity in the insula and amygdala was lower before pain (in the anticipation phase), while during pain activity in the insula and cingulate was, if anything, stronger; pain intensity was reported the same as by novices, and unpleasantness as less (Lutz et al. 2013). It is not that the signal stops sounding; it does not sound in the anticipation phase, and sounds when the event comes.

## How do idealization and trust differ?

<!--OPEN-->

Expectation is also a move of counting on the other as almighty, as seen in the chapter "[How do expectation and trust differ?](#ldd-expect)". Faith includes a way of believing in which the wish comes to the front, as seen in the chapter "[How do faith and trust differ?](#ldd-faith)". Both contain the move of choosing to see only the good side. On a page that made the five stages of creation into a diagram, pjdhiro named this move as a phenomenon to be especially careful of, and later restated it like this.

<a id="ldd-idealize"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Like Nonaka's three excesses, anything in excess is as bad as falling short. Self-destruction through excessive "good" is a phenomenon I feel around the various things that pass for "correct answers": one easily judges the fluctuation on the opposite side as "bad", chooses only the "good", and goes to excess and becomes fixed. Examples: justice, perfection, affection, value, being creative, knowledge, diversity, joy, power, peace.

On the same day, pjdhiro went on like this.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>I feel that even confident, idealistic policies have self-destruction through excessive "good".

Psychoanalysis has called the movement of selecting only the good aspects and seeing the opposite aspects split off idealization. Klein counted idealization among the early moves that protect against anxiety, and at the same time wrote that it is part of normal development (1946). The infant holds the satisfying object and the frustrating object apart, and protects itself from the terrifying aspect by enlarging the good aspect. What pjdhiro's fixing of "good", Klein's idealization and Nitta's *ate* have in common is a movement of selecting and enlarging the good aspect and keeping the opposite side split off.

Klein also writes that as the infant grows, the loved and hated aspects come to be felt as not so far apart as before. What wells up then is fear of loss, a strong sense of guilt and feelings close to mourning, and the urge to repair the one it feels it has harmed (reparation) comes to the fore. As anxiety weakens, Klein continues, the object is both less idealized and less terrifying.

This page does not settle on one difference between idealization and trust. Set beside idealization, one aspect of trust comes into view. The working notes of "What Is Awareness" take, as one of the axes for looking at trust, the side that sees only good outcomes and the side that holds good and bad together. ## How do reassurance and dependence differ from trust?

<!--OPEN-->

Idealization was a movement to protect oneself from anxiety. Then is the feeling of being protected, of relying on someone with reassurance, the same as trust? Around the time of writing the sister reader "Isn't Love like gravity?", pjdhiro looked back on a year of working with AI using the image of the umbilical cord (the chapter "[Isn't Love like gravity?](#ldd-center)") like this.

<a id="ldd-safety"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>For me, this year of collaborating with AI was exactly a sense of reassurance between me and a boundless AI, the brain of the world. Connected by an umbilical cord, entrusting my arguments with reassurance.

Winnicott wrote that the chief characteristic of infancy is dependence (1960). Wherever there is an infant there is always someone caring for it, and at first infant and care are one unit that cannot be separated. Winnicott called not only physically holding the infant but the whole of the provision surrounding the infant "holding", and wrote that it is reliable, not mechanically, but in a way that includes empathy for the other. Dependence is at first a dependence in which the infant is not even aware of the care, and later becomes a dependence aware of needing care. From there, Winnicott writes, as memories of care accumulate and a feeling of being able to rely on the environment (confidence) grows, the infant moves in the direction of managing without actual care.

In Winnicott's way of writing, relying and being held is the foothold for the feeling of being able to rely. Dependence can be read as not written as something discarded before reaching trust. Reassurance and dependence and trust can be written as two, but may not be separable. Whether trust always comes from accumulation is not decided on this page.

On the other hand, the same word "reassurance" is also sought from a different need. Maslow wrote of the need for safety as a need for a predictable, orderly, reliable world (1943). It includes preferring the familiar and the known, and looking for someone to protect one or a strong person to rely on. When, in the psychological safety section of the part on development, pjdhiro saw reassurance and safety sought in a spirit of self-protection or opportunism as a state like dependence through psychological defense mechanisms (the fold in the chapter "[Where are the words of business misunderstood?](#ldd-words)"), it was reassurance of this side. Both kinds of reassurance are expressions of people always relying on something somewhere, and cannot be sorted into good and bad. Set side by side, the two kinds of reassurance look different in what they seek protection from.

## What outlines do trust and reassurance show?

Aspects of trust touched:

- The side of its sources: some of the feeling of trust comes not only from the person in front of one, but from patterns one is not conscious of (Freud, Klein, Jung; the chapter "[Where do the feelings of trust and love come from?](#ldd-source)").
- Difference from expectation: expectation is the movement in which a self trying to be perfect counts on the other as omnipotent; trust, Shinya Nitta writes, wells up when the other supports one's fragility, and is born from relationships of mutual help.
- The side of acceptance: pjdhiro also calls self-acceptance trust in oneself, and sees it as the attitude of being convinced of oneself including what one cannot do and one's bad parts (the chapter "[How do acceptance and trust differ?](#how-do-acceptance-and-trust-differ)").
- Difference from faith: a way of believing in which the fulfilment of a wish comes to the fore (illusion) dispenses with verifying itself, Freud writes.
- Difference from idealization: idealization is the movement of selecting and enlarging only the good aspects and keeping the opposite side split off. On the axis in the working notes of "What Is Awareness" (the side that sees only good outcomes and the side that holds good and bad together), which side trust is on is not yet decided.
- The side of attachment: expectations about the other and oneself accumulated through repetition. Experiences of not being responded to are included too.
- Within the classification of emotions: where it is placed differs with the way of dividing, and it is written as something with a lasting length of time, different from something that wells up over a short time like love.
- How pjdhiro applies the word: pjdhiro feels something like a vector heading toward stability and integration, and applies "trust" to it. There are things the person feels as trust that others cannot call so.

Aspects of reassurance touched:

- Being able to be with anxiety: when one has accepted one's weakness, anxiety arises but one can be with it. pjdhiro sees this as one definition of reassurance (self-acceptance). pjdhiro also says, "It also resembles courage."
- Dependence and being held: relying and being held is the foothold for the feeling of being able to rely, Winnicott writes. Dependence is not written as something discarded before reaching trust.
- The need for safety: reassurance as a need for a predictable, orderly, reliable world (Maslow).
- Reassurance as protection: pjdhiro sees reassurance and safety sought in a spirit of self-protection or opportunism as a state like dependence through psychological defense mechanisms.
- Someone within: pjdhiro sees one's being able to hold a sense of something being off even with no one nearby as because there is someone one can trust within.
- The history of the word *anshin* (reassurance) is on the separate page "[What is anshin?](anshin.html)" (in Japanese; 🚧 under construction).

Trust and reassurance can be written as two, but may not be separable. With either, taking only one aspect overlooks another.

<details><summary>More — can the stability of being connected without confirming be measured?</summary>

<a id="ldd-love-measure"></a>In the hand-holding experiment, the person whose hand was held was visible and being touched. Then has the stability of being connected to someone unseen been measured? In part, it has.

Attachment research has seen people as holding, in their minds, images of others they can rely on. When they feel threatened, people reach, below the level of consciousness, for the image of someone not in front of them. Even just calling that image to mind brings reassurance, at least for a time. That even with someone unseen, an image in the mind brings reassurance has been shown by experiments up to this point.

However, what has been measured is the effect of images of others who can be named. There are also experiments that measured something close to an other given no name. When people were asked to record, moment by moment, how strongly they felt an invisible presence while watching footage of magnificent nature, the sense lasted from beginning to end, and afterward it was felt not as an agent like a god or Buddha, but as something like the air enveloping nature.

<a id="ldd-fujin"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>I express this by applying the word Fūjin, the wind god.

The other is an experiment that exposed people, without their noticing, to words suggesting the sacred. In a task of rearranging sentences, words such as spirit, divine and sacred were included in a form that would not be noticed. In both experiments the other was not visible and was given no name. However, the nature-footage experiment produced the sense by showing a grand landscape, and did not measure stability itself. Mikulincer, Gillath and Shaver (2002) showed that presenting words representing threat too fast to reach consciousness makes mental images (representations) of attachment figures more accessible. Takano and Nomura (2023) showed 77 university students two minutes each of footage of magnificent nature, funny animals and walking in a park, had them tilt a joystick when they felt an "invisible presence" while watching, and at the same time measured skin conductance and pupil size. <details><summary>More — how certain are intention, control and consciousness?</summary>

The word "business development" suggests the act of intentionally starting a business and moving it along as one wishes. Let us look at the three in turn from the side of research.

- **Intention**: Mintzberg and Waters (1983) distinguished strategies that were intended and realized, strategies that were intended but not realized, and strategies that took shape without being intended (emergent strategies). Mintzberg also wrote in 1994 that every strategy has some emergent quality.
- **Control**: Knight (1921) distinguished "risk", which can be estimated by probability, from "uncertainty", where even the probability is unknown. And Knight thought that business profit arises from taking on this uncertainty.
- **Consciousness**: Weick et al. (1999) wrote that people, after starting to act, use the results as clues to find out, looking back, what is happening. pjdhiro sees this as similar to "[dots connect looking backward](#ldd-dots)" in Jobs's speech. Wegner (1999, 2003) argued that the very feeling that "I did it" may be an interpretation the mind constructs by inferring from the relation between thought and action.

Lined up like this, "business development" may have the aspect of being a name for what happened, arranged afterward into a single story. At the same time, in Knight's distinction, business is also carried forward while taking on a future whose probabilities are unknown. This page asks about what did not make it into that story, and what was moving before the story. The same shape appears within one person, in a group of people, and in society.

##### How the supporting side engages (Takumi Miyamoto's interviews)

Separately from the four above, there is research that listens to affected people while having them draw, as a curve, the ups and downs of their feelings from the disaster to the present (Takumi Miyamoto's "recovery curves"). That person's curve rose at events and festivals of exchange with volunteers, things not directly tied to rebuilding their life.

Citing Kōichi Higo's argument, Miyamoto separates two kinds of engagement: "aiming" engagement, which tries to change the present for the better, and "being with" engagement, which recognizes the irreplaceability of the other on the premise that "you don't have to change". According to Miyamoto, directing only "aiming" engagement at someone during a period when they feel powerless denies their present and strengthens their sense of powerlessness. From the Kesennuma research, Miyamoto also writes that solving problems for affected people can instead take away their power.

Claude's reading: this research seems to shed light from the side on the question above, "Is it me who wants to bring about recovery?". It can be read that when the wish to push recovery forward is directed first from the supporting side, it can instead take away the affected people's power. The sequence of envisioning, planning and executing can be given a beginning and an end. Setting deadlines and drawing divisions lets one check progress. But those divisions are cut out by people from what is happening and attached to it. Processes not written in the sequence begin before the divisions and continue after them.

The beginning is in most cases seen afterward. As seen in the chapter "[What drives a project?](#ldd-question)", dots may connect looking back, as if preparation within one person had already begun. The field may fluctuate first, and one may start moving riding it.

The end is not settled as one either. "What Is Creation" places the bundle as the completion in which things are integrated as a new form. pjdhiro sees the bundle as having both aspects: completion or deliverables, and, as a direction or gathering, supporting the next field. pjdhiro also sees that a collision may not reach binding: one side may win, or things may return to the field and wait for the next opposition.

Coming undone, drifting apart and going back are also included in development on this page.

<details><summary>More — intended strategy, and strategy realized without being intended</summary>

The view that looks at both what is moved along intentionally and what takes shape without intention also exists in strategy research. In a 1983 chapter, Mintzberg and Waters gave four definitions of strategy. Two of them are strategy as a plan set out in advance, and strategy as a pattern seen in a stream of an organization's decisions and actions. Strategy as pattern, they write, is a definition concerned not with intention but with what was realized, that is, with what actually happened in the organization.

Combining these two definitions, they drew three strategies in one diagram: strategy intended and realized as intended; strategy intended but not realized; and strategy realized as a pattern without being particularly intended (emergent strategy).

What they followed were the strategies of organizations such as companies, newspapers, universities and government agencies. The range pjdhiro points to with the word development is not limited to organizations; it extends to change within one person, to families, and to the movements of society.

## What is this view good for?

<a id="ldd-use"></a><strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>So what's it good for? How do you use it?

<a id="ldd-screen"></a>In pjdhiro's view, vision, the words of the mind, and value are all names given to vortices born from the field, and vortices cannot be cut off from the field. So the way to use Love-Driven Development is to check what one has received before applying theories or instructions. pjdhiro sees Love-Driven Development as something that arranges the words and numbers received before thinking and deciding (the input), a sieve that asks whether one is treating vortices as vortices. The three chapters that follow look at this use in turn: when checking the words one has received (Where are the words of business misunderstood?), when giving something form (Where does design come in?), and what changes when it is used and how far it works (What changes when the input is arranged?). The first chapter deals with words used in business and management settings, so it limits the discussion to business. The other two take examples from business, but the discussion is not limited to business.

### Vision

Vision is usually spoken of as the starting point of a business. Then where does that vision come from?

<a id="ldd-vision"></a><strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>Vision may be something wonderful like wisdom, may be mere psychological defense, may be born at the end of action.

Where, why and how vision arises is still not well understood (the chapter "[What drives a project?](#ldd-question)"). pjdhiro sees that until the driving source working before it, what I want to call Love, is understood, what vision is may not be understood either. If one fixes its source as one thing while the 5W1H, structure and process of what is taken as vision are not understood, one thinks one understands, and the searching stops. In the terms of the chapter "[How do expectation and trust differ?](#ldd-expect)", expectation rides on the image of the future that a vision holds. This may be where vision moves people. Whether that image foresees what lies ahead or is maintained to protect oneself from anxiety is hard to tell apart from the content of the image alone.

pjdhiro's view on the word business conception being called disappointing is in the chapter "[How do expectation and trust differ?](#ldd-uncertain-practice)".

<details><summary>More — is vision psychological defense? (an image carrying expectation, and clues for telling apart)</summary>

pjdhiro sees whether something ends in disappointment as depending on ability and on the strength of psychological defense mechanisms. Following this view, if ability catches up with the image, the image becomes reality. If defense is strong, it can be read that one either keeps the image without admitting the betrayal (idealization), or stops moving just before being betrayed.

That uncertainty about future threat makes preparation hard, and that this leads to anxiety (Grupe & Nitschke, 2013), was seen in the fold of the chapter "[What is anxiety?](#ldd-uncertain)". When one feels the unseen ahead as a threat and the signal of survival is welling up, a vision may be easily sought as a picture that fills what lies ahead. It looks like the same shape as the misapplication of trying to solve a problem of trust by achieving survival.

Clues for telling apart come from the difference between expectation and trust.

- In pjdhiro's view, expectation is not something that sharpens estimates. If so, estimating is done with another tool. Kahneman and Tversky (1977) separated the view from the content of the plan at hand from the view from the distribution of outcomes of similar cases, and wrote that relying on the former tends to make plans optimistic. Whether the vision stays as it is when set beside the numbers of similar cases is one clue.
- Whether the image is made only of good things, or includes what does not fit. In the terms of the chapter "When signals and thoughts part, one feels a lack", a vision that keeps what does not fit held as a kesson can be read as holding good and bad together. Whether it can be called trust is not decided here, since what trust is is still being searched for. Idealization keeps the image by splitting off what does not fit.

If expectation is the energy to start moving, then a vision working as defense may also keep moving people. Taylor and Brown (1988), summarizing research, argued that the tendency to see oneself and the future as better than they are is a feature of ordinary thinking, and seems to support other aspects of mental health. There is also the aspect that without expectation one could not have started. In the sequence of recovery from social withdrawal that pjdhiro cites (the chapter "[How do expectation and trust differ?](#ldd-expect)"), expectation and conceit are also placed first. The person telling them apart also stands in the same uncertainty. So telling apart is not done once; it is repeated while moving.

Then where else, besides vision, can this view be used?

<details><summary>More — how do conflict, cognitive dissonance and the double bind differ?</summary>

All three look like two things in opposition. What differs is where, and what collides with what.

- **Conflict**: two or more motives or goals competing within one person. Lewin divided this into three types: wanting both (approach–approach), wanting to avoid both (avoidance–avoidance), and being both drawn to and wanting to avoid one goal (approach–avoidance). Discrepancies of wishes, opinions or norms between people are also called interpersonal conflict (*Saishin shinrigaku jiten*).
- **Cognitive dissonance**: a state within one person in which things one knows or believes do not fit with one another. The misfit produces pressure to reduce it, and people may reduce it by changing their own thoughts or attitudes (*Dejitaru Daijisen*; Festinger and Carlsmith 1959). In Festinger and Carlsmith's experiment, people made to tell others that a boring task had been interesting, and paid one dollar, later themselves rated the task as interesting. Those paid twenty dollars did not change their ratings. It is written that they persuaded themselves to fit what they had said.

## Where does design come in?

<details class="pd-column" id="ldd-thinking"><summary><span>Types of thinking, and design rules<span class="pd-column__sub">Logical, process, systems and design thinking, and Baldwin and Clark's modularity</span></span></summary>

Talk of design often brings up ways of thinking that carry the name "thinking", such as design thinking. There are also names like logical thinking, process thinking and systems thinking. Each can be seen as a name given to where one turns one's eyes within the single activity of thinking. Here, what each has been written about as a way of seeing is lined up briefly from representative sources. Which sources were chosen is this page's arrangement.

- **Logical thinking**: spoken of as a way of thinking that traces the connection from grounds to conclusions. In chapter 1 of *How We Think* (1910), the philosopher Dewey called "reflective thought" the active, persistent and careful consideration of a belief in the light of the grounds that support it and the further conclusions to which it tends.
- **Process thinking**: a way of thinking that sees things not as a collection of static individuals but as processes of becoming. The "Process Philosophy" entry of the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* writes that it is a philosophy based on the premise that being is dynamic and that the dynamic nature of being should be the primary focus of any account of reality. The same entry also writes that Western metaphysics has long described reality as a collection of static individuals.
- **Systems thinking**: a way of thinking that looks at the relations between parts, and at the whole, rather than at the parts. The "What is Systems Thinking?" entry of the Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge (SEBoK), citing Senge, writes of it as a discipline for seeing wholes, a framework for seeing interrelationships rather than things. The same entry also writes that there are many examples where drawing the boundaries of scope or time too narrowly solves the present problem but creates the same or a larger problem elsewhere or later.
- **Design thinking**: IDEO's page describes design thinking as a human-centered approach to solving complex problems, in the form of bringing together what is desirable for people, what is viable for the organization, and what is technically feasible. Design thinking is also placed in the table of the separate page "[How far do the ideas of management studies work?](management-drivers.html#デザイン思考)" (in Japanese).

A piece of work that thought about "rules" on the side of design is Baldwin and Clark's *Design Rules, Vol. 1: The Power of Modularity* (2000). Clark was then dean of Harvard Business School. In a conversation the same year, the two named IBM's System/360, launched in the early 1960s, as the first modular family of computers: the parts of the computer could be designed independently by separate specialist teams, and as long as they followed a set of design rules decided in advance, they worked as one when connected.

As long as the rules are followed, what the designers of other parts need not see can be changed freely. The two call this "hidden design parameters". Baldwin also says that such modularity does not arise by chance but has to be designed in. Knowing the rules, anyone can make parts that work in the system. The two name as a major event in the growth of Silicon Valley that after System/360, designers of disk drives left IBM one after another to start their own companies and compete with compatible devices.

Read in this page's words, design rules look closer to arrangements on the side of the field where vortices arise than to the vortices that are each part. This is Claude's reading. In the background of this reader, too, pjdhiro likens a year of work to [refactoring into modules](#ldd-my-app).

Then what moves thinking itself? In the same chapter 1, Dewey placed the origin of thinking in some perplexity, confusion or doubt. He also wrote that thinking is not a case of spontaneous combustion, that there is something that occasions it. pjdhiro sees it this way.

<strong>pjdhiro's view (translated by Claude):</strong>The driving source of thinking is a mystery too. It might be Love. Desire for knowledge, or deficiency, for the sake of a sense of reassurance.

<details><summary>More — (2) biases decrease (the error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete, and ways to reduce it)</summary>

<a id="ldd-use-2"></a>One is less often misled by things easy to perceive, like vortices. pjdhiro sees it as a way of dealing with cognitive distortions and limits. On this page, the fold "[Words are names for the vortices seen between the waves](#ldd-vortex)" in the chapter "What shape does creation take?" saw that what people gave names to were shapes easy to perceive. Errors arise easily where a named vortex is treated as a thing cut off from the field.

In chapter 3 of *Science and the Modern World* (1925), the philosopher Whitehead named the error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete "the fallacy of misplaced concreteness". In the same passage Whitehead also wrote that the intellect need not fall into this trap. The direction is not to discard abstractions but to use them knowing they are abstractions. The difference between giving a vortex a name and treating a named vortex as a thing has the same shape as this distinction.

In psychology, ways of reducing bias have been studied. In 2015 Morewedge and colleagues reported that a single training session, with a game or a video, reduced six biases, including confirmation bias and anchoring, by medium to large amounts, and that the effect remained two months later.

The same thing has been said in investment practice. In 2000 Barber and Odean examined the trades of more than 66,000 households and showed that the households that traded most earned an annual return of 11.4%, well below the market's 17.9%, and that this can be explained by overconfidence. In a 1994 talk, the investor Charlie Munger cited the saying "to a man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail", and recommended holding the ways of thinking of many fields combined like a latticework. It is an example of speaking of increasing viewpoints and reducing bias as one measure.

In consciousness research, metacognition is written of as a higher-order process that takes cognition as its object (Overgaard and Sandberg, 2012): the work of looking at what one is thinking as an object of thought.

Research on adult development writes that what at one stage one sees from within and cannot take as an object comes to be seen as an object at the next stage. O'Fallon defines a stage as a perspective, and counts it by which person-perspective one can take (2020, p.18; the chapter "[What looks threatening changes with the stage of development](#ldd-stages)"). One's own thoughts and values, too, are invisible while one sees from within them, and become visible when they become objects. One's self, too, gradually comes to be seen as an object in this transition.

What comes to be seen as an object at each stage is laid out, as a summary of O'Fallon's definitions, in the table "What can and cannot be taken as an object" in [What are stages of mental development?](developmental-stages.html#何を対象にできるかできないか) (in Japanese). Cook-Greuter's descriptions of the stages also show the handling of others' views stage by stage. At the Expert stage, losing one's current certainty by opening to others' views becomes a fear (2013, pp.39–40). At the Pluralist stage, many perspectives are visible, and it is written that when fairness and respect for voices are too strong, one becomes unable to decide for fear of being seen as one-sided (pp.53, 58–59). For people at the earlier stage, taking others' perspectives amounts to going beyond fear and seeing what could not yet be taken as an object; for people who already see many perspectives, it can also amount to staying where they are.

<details><summary>More — the question of the "between" in human, space and time</summary>

At the root of the view of dividing oneself, pjdhiro places the following question ([chapter 6](gravity-love.html#gl-roots) of the sister reader "Isn't Love like gravity?", in Japanese).

<strong>pjdhiro's question (translated by Claude):</strong>What if a human (*ningen*) exists in the between of person and person, and the self (*jibun*) exists where one has divided oneself? I think a question like that is at the root. The word *jibun* expresses exactly that.

(In Japanese, *ningen* (human) is written with the characters for "person" and "between", and *jibun* (self) with the characters for "oneself" and "part, divide".)

This question is within the picture of the gravity well. As seen in the fold "[Placing the explorations so far on the single picture of the gravity well](#ldd-summary)" in the chapter "What is what I want to call Love like?", pjdhiro sees oneself, standing in the hollow, as being among people. The four explorations lined up in this reader can be read as lying on the line of dividing oneself within one person. Whether several centers orbiting one another's wells can be written with the same line is not yet known.

The three Japanese words for human (*ningen*), space (*kūkan*) and time (*jikan*) all contain the character 間, "between". Asked what pjdhiro feels in the between of person and person, pjdhiro gave the following experience.

<a id="ldd-between"></a><strong>pjdhiro's experience (translated by Claude):</strong>Other people's feelings and viewpoints sometimes appear inside me, as if contagious, as if copied. Before I think it, it has already happened. For example, it isn't like looking at someone and thinking "they look sad"; without noticing, it has become "I am sad", and I notice afterward. It may be an illusion, but that's how I feel it. Atmosphere, or sensing, or empathy — as if there were something in the between of person and person.

Psychology and neuroscience have called something close to this way of appearing emotional contagion. De Vignemont and Singer (2006) defined empathy narrowly: a state in which one's own emotion is isomorphic to the other's emotion, and one knows that the other is its source. On that basis, they separate emotional contagion as a state in which emotion is shared but self and other are not distinguished. Their example is an infant who starts crying when other infants cry. pjdhiro's experience can be read as a flow in which an emotion that appeared without distinction is distinguished afterward (Claude's reading). The record of checking this theory is in the intersubjectivity record of "[What Is Awareness](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/as/)" (in Japanese).

<!--REF:Nerves and emotion-->

Sources: de Vignemont & Singer (2006) "[The empathic brain: how, when and why?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2006.08.008)", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 10(10):435–441 (full text checked against the authors' manuscript in the institutional repository HAL; the four conditions of empathy, and that emotional contagion does not meet the condition of distinguishing self and other p.435).

About space and time too, pjdhiro has experiences of what had been divided wavering.

<strong>pjdhiro's experience (translated by Claude):</strong>With time and space too, reading the theory of relativity breaks down my common sense. What I naively thought was space turns out to be something like time, and what I thought was time turns out to be something like space.

In special relativity, how much of the separation between two events counts as time and how much as space changes with the motion of the observer. Two events that happen at the same time in separate places for one observer happen at different times for an observer moving relative to them. So this theory writes space and time combined into one, as spacetime.

Claude reads the two experiences as follows. Both happen where the division between things one had seen as divided is not as firm as one thought. People are separate bodies, but emotions sometimes appear between them before a distinction is made. Space and time, too, are counted separately in everyday life, but how they are divided changes with the motion of the observer. This page has looked at what I want to call Love as a field before division. Human, space and time may be three "betweens" that become visible when people divide that field.

</details>

## How I came to this question — background and history

<a id="ldd-background"></a>From here on is the scaffolding of the main text: how this view came about, and where things were cited from.

<a id="ldd-name"></a>The name Love-Driven Development was given afterward to events encountered again and again. The name came first, and this year has been a search for what it is that I want to call by that name. The course of it is placed in the folds below.

<details><summary>Looking for the driving source</summary>

Because phenomena similar to the five stages of creation are common across fields, "What Is Creation" has researched similar things in [30 academic domains](https://uminomae.github.io/project-design-theory/cs/?modal=domains) (in Japanese). In the course of looking for the driving source, when trying to picture what moves things behind the sea and the waves, applying the words nothingness, zero or emptiness only caused more confusion, and pjdhiro was looking for a shape that could be grasped with the eyes and plain senses.

<strong>pjdhiro's request (translated by Claude):</strong>A schematic diagram of the universe that draws gravity in 3D as a distortion in the z direction. What if, at its sun-like center, there were the concept of love? That the place that does not move is the driving engine. A place that is nothingness, zero, where seeing itself is hard to see from itself. Draw that.

From this request were born the picture of the [gravity well](gravity-well.html) and the reader "[Isn't Love like gravity?](gravity-love.html)" (both in Japanese).

</details>

<details><summary>From emotion processing to what is not two</summary>

While looking for the driving source, pjdhiro was seeing phenomena in which people's minds seem to act on one another unconsciously, and movements of emotion that proceed neither logically nor rationally. To deal with these, pjdhiro worked on something like a mechanism for processing emotions: the emotion processing of Project Design Theory.

In that work, pjdhiro met what is not two (non-duality), and the yardsticks of good and bad gradually came off. <a id="ldd-shingyo"></a>The clue was the Heart Sutra. With "不生不滅 不垢不浄 不増不減" (neither arising nor ceasing, neither defiled nor pure, neither increasing nor decreasing), the Heart Sutra negates one by one the pairs of arising and ceasing, defiled and pure, increasing and decreasing. It can be read as the form of ceasing to stand on either of the two that were divided.

Sources: *Prajñāpāramitā Heart Sutra*, Xuanzang's translation (Taishō vol. 8 No.251, p.848c, "不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減"; full text checked against [the CBETA electronic text](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/T0251_001)).

The hypothesis "Might there be no good and bad, no rationality?" in the chapter "[What is still not known](#ldd-open)" came out of this history.

</details>

Writing something whose inside cannot be seen from its behavior.
